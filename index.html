About

Singapore

I work at the intersection of operations research, revenue management, and online algorithms, designing real-time decision rules with provable guarantees against unpredictable demand.

My research studies robust real-time decision-making under uncertain demand, spanning resource allocation, order scheduling, and dynamic pricing. The focus is on algorithms whose performance is guaranteed even when arrivals are adversarial or hard to forecast.

A second strand of my work combines real-time decisions with machine-learned predictions. The resulting algorithms exploit accurate forecasts when they are available and remain resilient when they are not.

Miscellaneous

On investing and uncertainty

I believe in long-term value investing. To borrow Benjamin Graham’s metaphor, over long horizons the market is a weighing machine: prices tend to follow the value a business creates. Over short horizons, anything can happen, because the market is a voting machine: prices move with sentiment and with rare, high-impact events that nobody saw coming. Nassim Nicholas Taleb’s Fooled by Randomness and The Black Swan shaped how I think about this, and I recommend both highly.

This view is also part of why I study real-time decisions under adversarial arrivals and imperfect predictions. Deciding how to invest in a chaotic market is, at heart, a resource allocation problem:

Resource
The capital you can deploy.
Steady rise
Low-class customers arriving: each opportunity pays a modest return.
Sharp crash
High-class customers arriving: capital deployed near the bottom earns far more once prices recover.
The catch
The order of arrivals is unknown and may be adversarial.

Commit everything during the rally and nothing is left when the crash comes. Hold everything back and the steady gains pass you by. So the question is the one my research asks: how should we plan our positions without knowing how the market will move? Protection levels are one answer: keep enough in reserve for the rare, valuable opportunities while still serving the ones in front of you.

Anything can happen, in any industry, in any market, and in life. We cannot predict it, but we can prepare for it as well as possible.
